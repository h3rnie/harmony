Beyond what the challenge asks. The Global pulse plays every month since November 2000 on a globe rendered with WebGPU; this frame is September 2019. Two more modes show what only a harmonized record can: a fire clock, the planet coloured by the month it normally burns, and a trend map for 2001 to 2025 that reproduces the published decline in savanna burning and the rise in Canadian boreal fire, with no step at the sensor change. A watchlist scans the planet each month for places burning unusually. You can listen to the record: the raw sensors jump in pitch when VIIRS arrives, the harmonized record does not, which also gives blind and low-vision users a way in. A live layer reads the last week as a pace against normal through a server-side proxy, so the NASA key never reaches the browser. And an AI brief turns the statistics into a paragraph a duty officer can read aloud, answering follow-up questions only from the numbers on the page.
