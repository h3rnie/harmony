This is the live app. A responder picks an area by drawing it, typing coordinates, or choosing from a watchlist of places burning unusually this month. The first thing on screen is the answer: here, September 2020 in the Gran Chaco was 1.4 times its normal, the fourth most active September in 26 years. Below it the page follows the questions a newcomer asks, in order, starting with the burning calendar the challenge asks for: one row per year, one column per month, all on one scale, in colours that work for colour-blind readers. Everything is computed in the browser by a Rust kernel compiled to WebAssembly, reading small static tiles, so the answer arrives in about a second. The app also reads the last week as a pace against normal through a server-side proxy, writes a brief a duty officer can read aloud, plays the record as sound for people who cannot see the chart, shows the whole planet on a WebGPU globe, and exports the hotspots behind any search as a CSV.
