This is what a responder sees. Draw an area, type coordinates, or pick one from the watchlist of places burning unusually this month. The first thing on screen is the answer: here, September 2020 in the Gran Chaco was 1.4 times its normal, the fourth most active September in 26 years. Below it, the page follows the questions a newcomer asks, in order, starting with the burning calendar the challenge asks for: one row per year, one column per month, all on the same scale. Everything is computed in the browser by a Rust kernel compiled to WebAssembly, and the hotspots behind any search download as a CSV.
