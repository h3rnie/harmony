Harmony is live at harmony.hernie.me, and the code, the method and the tests are public on GitHub. The globe on the right is from the app: every month since November 2000, rendered in the browser. The record is built entirely on NASA FIRMS data, and because it weights each satellite by its measured error it carries on with NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 after MODIS retires. It is for three groups. Responders get a plain answer to “is this unusual?”, in the same unit as the last 26 years. Scientists get a validated series with its uncertainty and its limits stated. Land managers get the rhythm of a place: when it burns, how that is shifting, which years to learn from. We acknowledge the use of data and imagery from NASA’s Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), part of NASA’s Earth Observing System Data and Information System (EOSDIS). This work is dedicated to everyone who works the fire line, the dispatch desk and the long night after. I am Toh Yu Heng, team Hernie, from Singapore. Thank you.
