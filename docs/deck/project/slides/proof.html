Does it work? I hold out each year from 2012 to 2021 in turn, calibrate on the rest, and compare with what MODIS actually saw. At the scale of a one-degree cell the correlation is 0.944 and the overall bias is 0.06 percent. A change-point test finds an 8.9-fold step in the naive record at the hand-over; in the harmonized record it is 7 percent and not statistically significant. Because MODIS is still flying, 2022 to 2025 are a free test on years the model never saw: within 1.5 percent for three years, then 7 percent in 2025 as the MODIS orbits drift. I also trained a small neural network as a challenger. It is slightly more accurate, it runs in the browser on WebGPU, and it agrees with the simple method, which is why the simple, explainable method stays in production. Limits are published in the app: detections are not burned area, and cloud and overpass gaps are not corrected.
