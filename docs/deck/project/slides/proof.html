Two kinds of evidence. First, accuracy: holding out each year from 2012 to 2021 in turn, the harmonized record correlates 0.944 with what MODIS saw at one-degree scale. A change-point test finds a 12.6-fold step in the naive record at the hand-over, and no significant step after harmonization. On unseen years, 71 to 81 percent of months fall inside the stated 80 percent range, so the ranges are a few points optimistic and we say so. Second, a finding. Because VIIRS keeps a fixed overpass time, it can be used as a reference to measure MODIS itself. Since 2020 Terra’s orbit has drifted 1.1 hours earlier, and its daytime fire detections now run 42 percent low; at night they run 35 percent high. Aqua’s daytime detections are unchanged. In the years before the drift the same ratios sit at one. NASA lists the effect of this drift on fire products as under investigation; to our knowledge this is a global, per-biome estimate of it. The limit: it cannot be separated from real changes in the hour fires burn. Fusion beat either single sensor at one-degree scale, though not a plain average, and a small neural model run in the browser agrees with the simple method.
