7

Voice

Taglines, one sentence of mission, where the motto lives, and a dedication.

A · Mission control + patch

Taglines
  1. Two sensors. One fire record.
  2. Every fire season since 2000, on one scale.
  3. Know when the land burns.
Mission

Harmony joins two generations of fire-detecting satellite sensors into one consistent record, so anyone responsible for a landscape can see when it burns, which years were extreme, and how this month compares with normal.

Motto and spirit

Ad astra per aspera appears only on the patch: the intro screen, the about panel, the closing slide, the video end card. The interface never says anything heroic. Its precision is the tribute.

Dedication

For everyone who works the fire line, the dispatch desk and the long night after.

B · Mission patch throughout

Taglines
  1. Watching fire for the people who face it.
  2. Twenty-six years of fire, told as one story.
  3. Two generations of satellites, one record for the ground.
Mission

Satellites have watched Earth's fires for twenty-six years; Harmony turns that watch into one record that a crew lead, a planner or a scientist can read in a minute.

Motto and spirit

Ad astra per aspera rides the emblem's ring and is translated once, where people first meet it: through hardship, to the stars. The human spirit shows up as people in the copy (crews, dispatchers, neighbours), never as heroic adjectives.

Dedication

Dedicated to the firefighters, dispatchers and land stewards who read the land so the rest of us can sleep.

C · Modernist poster

Taglines
  1. One record. Every fire season since 2000.
  2. 26 years. 12 months. One scale.
  3. Fire, on the record.
Mission

Harmony is one fire record for the whole planet: two satellite generations, one scale, twenty-six years.

Motto and spirit

Set once, small, as the colophon under the giant wordmark: Ad astra per aspera. Scale and order carry the confidence, so the words never have to strain.

Dedication

This record is for the people on the fire line.

Trade-off

What each direction costs.

A · Mission control + patch

Lowest risk and nearly free to adopt, since it is today's interface sharpened. The warmth lives only in a few patch moments, so a judge who sees just the app may find it cool.

B · Mission patch throughout

The warmest story and the strongest emblem, but navy fights the fixed ramps (grey midpoint, dark inferno end), and the illustration-led look can read as nostalgia rather than precision.

C · Modernist poster

The most memorable on a projector, but a light-first, big-type system fights a dense globe and calendar, and its red-orange shares a hue with the hottest data.

Recommendation

Choose A. Harmony's case to judges rests on Validity as much as Presentation, and A keeps the calm instrument the team has already built and tuned around colour-blind-safe data: the neutral ground lets both ramps read exactly as designed, and the stepped H explains the product's core idea, two records joined across their overlap, in four rectangles. The patch then carries everything the brief asks for on the human side (the motto, the 26 seasons, the dedication) in the moments where people should feel it: the intro, the about panel, the deck and the video end card. It is also the only direction the team can finish properly before judging.

  • A + BKeep A's interface and stepped H, and use B's illustrated emblem in place of A's line patch for the intro, deck and video. More warmth on stage, no cost to the data.
  • A + CKeep A's interface, and build the slide deck on C's poster grid and very large type, recoloured to ember on near-black. The most presentation punch for the least app work.
